Victor Hugo and Notre-Dame 📖
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 📖

Stand for a moment outside Notre-Dame and look up at the towers. Somewhere above the square, Victor Hugo placed a bell ringer called Quasimodo. A familiar Paris landmark becomes the setting for a story about how we judge other people.

Hugo's The Hunchback of Notre-Dame asks us to look beyond appearances. In Les Misérables, he explores kindness, poverty and justice through Jean Valjean's life. His characters help explain why literature has such an important place in French culture.

Three words for your next French Atelier conversation:

🇫🇷 un personnage : a character
🇫🇷 une histoire : a story
🇫🇷 un écrivain : a writer

Fun fact: the French title of The Hunchback of Notre-Dame is Notre-Dame de Paris. The cathedral gives the novel its name.

Which character from a book has stayed with you? Try naming one and saying a little about them in French. Ask your French Atelier teacher for a word you need, without having to explain the whole plot.

B2-2 · Amélie · Montmartre

French Atelier WhatsApp Business
Explore Montmartre with Amélie 🎬
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 🎬

Take a short walk through Montmartre. You pass a café window, hear cups settling onto saucers and watch someone stop to greet a neighbour. It is an ordinary Paris morning, the sort of small moment Amélie invites us to notice.

In the 2001 film, Audrey Tautou plays a waitress who quietly tries to improve other people's lives. Director Jean-Pierre Jeunet's colourful Montmartre helped make the neighbourhood known beyond France. It shows French cinema's playful side.

Three words to try between French Atelier classes:

🇫🇷 un quartier : a neighbourhood
🇫🇷 un voisin : a neighbour
🇫🇷 un sourire : a smile

Fun fact: the Café des 2 Moulins, where Amélie works in the film, is a real café on rue Lepic.

Think of a small kindness you noticed recently. Could you describe the person or the place in French? Start with one short sentence. Your French Atelier teacher can help you find the words to add a little more detail.

French Atelier WhatsApp Business
200 years of photography 📷
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 📷

Try framing a view through a window. A roof, a courtyard, a patch of sky. Today you can capture it in a moment. Two centuries ago, making a lasting image was an experiment.

In 2026, France begins celebrating 200 years of photography. Nicéphore Niépce's view from a window at Le Gras, dated 1826 or 1827, is recognised as the oldest surviving camera photograph. Everyday surroundings became part of a remarkable French invention.

Three words for describing a photograph in French:

🇫🇷 un appareil photo : a camera
🇫🇷 la lumière : light
🇫🇷 une image : an image

Fun fact: Niépce called his process heliography, meaning writing with the sun.

Choose a photograph you like and name one thing you can see. It is a simple way to practise before your next French Atelier class. Start with a short sentence.

B2-5 · The French tricolour

French Atelier WhatsApp Business
Why the French flag has three colours 🇫🇷
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 🇫🇷

You are in a French town square as people gather for a celebration. Above the crowd, blue, white and red flags move in the breeze. You recognise the country immediately, but the colours also tell a story.

During the French Revolution, blue and red, associated with Paris, were brought together with white, associated with the monarchy. The tricolour grew from that meeting of colours and became a lasting symbol of France.

Three words to notice next time you see it:

🇫🇷 un drapeau : a flag
🇫🇷 une couleur : a colour
🇫🇷 la liberté : freedom

Fun fact: the flag's present arrangement was adopted in 1794, with blue nearest the flagpole, white in the middle and red at the outer edge.

Look around your own neighbourhood. Which colours appear on signs, doors or clothing? Naming them is a small French exercise you can do anywhere. At French Atelier, you can bring those everyday observations into a conversation with your teacher, one simple sentence at a time.

B2-6 · Claude Monet · Musée Marmottan Monet

French Atelier WhatsApp Business
See light differently with Monet 🎨
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 🎨

Walk through the Musée Marmottan Monet in Paris and pause before Impression, Sunrise. A small orange sun hangs over the harbour. Boats and reflections emerge from loose touches of colour rather than sharp outlines.

Claude Monet was interested in how a place looks at a particular moment. Light changes, water moves, and the same view can feel different a little later. Looking closely at his painting gives you something simple to describe, even with a few French words.

Try these in your next French Atelier conversation:

🇫🇷 la lumière : light
🇫🇷 un reflet : a reflection
🇫🇷 un paysage : a landscape

Fun fact: the title of Impression, Sunrise inspired the name Impressionism after the painting appeared in an exhibition in 1874.

Look out of a window now. Is the light bright or soft? Can you see a reflection? Choose one detail and describe it aloud. Your French Atelier teacher can help you find the words to describe what you see.

B2-7 · Quiche Lorraine

French Atelier WhatsApp Business
Make a quiche in Lorraine 🥧
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 🥧

In a cooking class in Lorraine, in northeastern France, you press pastry into a tin while small pieces of bacon sizzle nearby. On the table, eggs and cream are ready to become the filling.

As the quiche bakes, the pastry turns golden and a savoury aroma fills the room. Your first bite brings crisp edges and a soft, creamy centre. It is a useful moment to connect French words with something you can touch, smell and taste.

Three ingredients to remember:

🇫🇷 la pâte : pastry
🇫🇷 les œufs : eggs
🇫🇷 la crème : cream

Fun fact: traditional quiche Lorraine is made without cheese. The familiar combination is pastry, eggs, cream and bacon.

Think about a dish you prepare at home. Can you name three ingredients in French? Practise saying them before your next French Atelier class, then ask your teacher about any words you are missing. There is no need to explain the whole recipe; a short description is a good place to start.

B2-8 · Tour de France · The Pyrenees

French Atelier WhatsApp Business
Follow the Tour into the Pyrenees 🚴
Bonjour {{BODY_1}} 🚴

You're waiting beside a mountain road in the Pyrenees. People clap as the first riders round a bend. Behind them, the road drops towards the valley. For spectators, a Tour de France stage is also a day spent together outdoors.

Look for the distinctive jerseys in the group. Yellow marks the overall leader, green the points leader, and white with red polka dots the leader of the mountains classification. Each colour tells you something different about the race.

Three words to practise before your next French Atelier class:

🇫🇷 un vélo : a bicycle
🇫🇷 un sommet : a summit
🇫🇷 un maillot : a jersey

Fun fact: the Tour first took riders into the high mountains of the Pyrenees in 1910.

Families and friends gather along the route to cheer the riders on. Would you choose a mountain stage or a finish in town? Try giving one reason in French. Your teacher can help you describe the setting, even if cycling is new to you.
